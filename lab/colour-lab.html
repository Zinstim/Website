Six colour directions on the same brand pieces: the mark, the buttons, the drop, the pour, the categories and the waitlist. Deep Zinc is the decision — the others are what it was tested against. Switch between them, hover or tap everything, or mix your own at the bottom.

The three cuts

Deep carbon, unchanged — glow, crimson button, teal states — with Taskin's diagonal added. The only difference between the three is the stripe: silver, crimson, or both.

Where it lands

Cut in both is the recommendation: it is the jar itself — silver lid, crimson mark, black body — and the two values keep the diagonal reading as two materials rather than one wide band.

The measurements

Silver stripe 5.3:1, crimson stripe 3.4:1, both are structure rather than text. Crimson stays the action colour, and the button is still the reddest thing on the page.

      Who read it better

      Your friends were right about the feel, and Gemini was right about the details. Neither was right on its own. Every number below was re-measured, not taken on trust.

      Hridoy and Taskin

      • “Darkish, deep.” Right. Four of your five mood board images are near-black at night, and a red logo glows on dark but goes flat on pale grey. Today’s site is 60% light Zinc Wash, and that is the biggest miss.
      • “Our logo is red, so build from it.” Right. The brand’s strongest asset is hiding inside a black mark.
      • “The first look has to pull people in.” Right. On a waitlist-only site, the first screen is the whole pitch.
      • The Orbyta gradient. A digital-agency look with no link to zinc, gyms or the red mark, and it fights the logo (try it above). White text dies on its light corner at 2.2:1. It is also the most common generated look right now.
      • The ANZO red, as it is. Great for brake parts, but for a gym brand it is the category default (ASRV, the supplement aisle). Small red text on black fails at 4.2:1.
      • “Neon” is the right instinct in the wrong colours. The on-brand version is light glowing in the dark, like the taillights in your own mood board.

      Gemini

      • The right red. Your mark’s cool crimson, not fire red. Its distance maths is correct: I recomputed all 14 CIEDE2000 values and every one matches.
      • The constraints that will save you. Crimson cannot carry small text on black (3.35:1). Red and teal look the same to colour-blind readers. Very dark crimson crushes to black on cheap phones in sunlight. Garment dye mutes colour.
      • Numbers. OKLCH is exact apart from one colour, and APCA is within about 3 Lc of mine (version drift). The conclusions hold.
      • It repeated the brief. It never tested the obvious alternative your friends raised: a dark site.
      • Unsourced competitor colours. “Gymshark #D32F2F” is Google’s stock Material Design red 700. The maths was right, but the inputs were guesses.
      • Its colour stream breaks your rules. A full-bleed dark-red drop band, a pulsing cursor (a loop), and crimson buttons on a pale base that read like sale buttons.

      The cut: which stripe wins

      • Deep carbon is the base, untouched. The crimson glow, the crimson button, teal for live states. Only the diagonal is new. (Kash)
      • Cut in both — the recommendation. Silver leading, oxblood behind: it is the jar itself, and the two values read as two materials instead of one thick band.
      • Cut in silver. The calmest and the most “instrument”. At 5.3:1 the stripe is the brightest thing on the page, so keep it out of the text column.
      • Cut in crimson. The most energy, and the closest to the aisle: black, red and a diagonal is ASRV’s signature, and red ends up doing three jobs at once.
      • Dark ground repairs the old collision. Red-led was killed on light, where error red sits 0.033 from the mark’s crimson. On Night the error is #FF7A6B, 0.208 away.
      • Still to prove. The crimson on a cheap Android in sunlight, and a thread match before the red is locked for Wear.

      What to do next

      1. Pick a cut, then build it on one real page first — the homepage hero, the drop and the join band — before touching the rest. Deep Zinc stays in this page as the fallback if the red ever feels like too much.
      2. Check the mark on a cheap Android in daylight, and buy a crimson thread and a garment-dyed swatch before locking the red for Wear.
      3. Send this page to Hridoy and Taskin so they can see where their notes landed, and read the brand book for the rules that came out of it.
