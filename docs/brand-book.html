Ten rules

  1. Quiet is the position. Every rival sells the shout. We sell the small correct input, repeated after anyone stopped watching.
  2. Night for the brand, Day for the proof. Obsidian carries the story; Zinc Wash carries sizes, doses, lab panels and policy.
  3. One live colour. The elixir teal means alive, measured, recovering, correct. Under 8% of any screen.
  4. Red acts, and only in the dark. Crimson leads the things you touch on Night. On a light surface red means failure, so nothing there is crimson but the mark.
  5. Structure is silver. The stripes, plates and rules are the jar lid's own metal — that is what keeps black-and-red from becoming the supplement aisle.
  6. Colour is an event. One crimson action per screen, teal under 3%. If two coloured things fight for attention, one of them is wrong.
  7. Dots mean where you are. Liquid means what you do. Those are the only two motion grammars.
  8. Gradients live inside liquid, and in one bloom. Light may glow on the dark behind a headline. Surfaces still get no gradient fills — zinc is a solid.
  9. One type family, pushed to both extremes. Archivo narrow and heavy for display, normal for reading. Bangla sits beside English, never beneath it.
  10. Nothing loops. Motion answers a person. It never performs on its own.
  11. Say what the thing is. If a line could sit on a competitor's site with the logo swapped, rewrite it.

01

The brand

ZinStim is a fitness brand from Dhaka that sells confidence, and treats the product as the proof rather than the pitch.

Wear

What you put on. Training tees first, cut and weight stated in grams, not adjectives.

Fuel

What you take in. Creatine and protein, with an independent lab panel published beside each batch.

Instruments

What measures it. Watches and bands. Not open yet: an empty door is the first promise a brand cannot keep.

Who it is for

Bangladesh first, the diaspora second, and the order matters. Bangla sits beside English with equal standing, not as a translation toggle. For readers abroad, being built in Dhaka is the asset, not the thing to soften.

Where it is now

Story first, waitlist only, nothing for sale. No transaction means no regulatory dependency, so the brand can be alive months before the product ships. It also matches the argument: the thing worth having is the thing built slowly. The waitlist is the only button on the site.

02

The name

Zin — from zinc

Element 30. A trace mineral: your body holds about two grams of it, and that is the whole supply. It runs protein synthesis, testosterone, immune response and recovery. It is the smallest correct thing in the system, and nobody thanks it.

Stim — from stimulus

The training stress that causes the body to adapt: stimulus, recovery, adaptation. Deliberately stimulus, not stimulant. We are not in the caffeine business, and the brand never sells a jolt.

Put together: the small correct input that causes a change. The name is the thesis, which is why the brand can be quiet everywhere else.

03

The idea: Trace

Change is not made of dramatic gestures. It is made of small correct inputs, repeated past the point where anyone is watching.

Why this idea holds

It comes out of the name instead of being applied to it. It makes the sparse layouts mean something: sparse because the subject is trace, not sparse because there was nothing to put there. And it gives us the one thing the category does not have — a position that cannot be copied by turning the volume up.

The one moment

Zinc is explained exactly once on the whole site, as a full-bleed divider. After that it is never mentioned again: no atomic numbers, no periodic table, no lab language anywhere else.

Two grams of it.
You never noticed.

The test

Every other brand in this aisle sells the gesture. We sell the opposite, and it is the more honest of the two.

Confidence, correctly defined: shouting is insecurity at volume. Confidence is quiet, specific and unbothered — which is also what the layout looks like.

04

The mark

A swept blade-form Z, drawn as one closed silhouette in a 128 × 114 box. It is a letter, a tool and a vessel at the same time.

Anatomy

The blade is the long stroke, its axis at 45°: the stimulus, the cut, the training stress. The wedge answers it at 26°, low and to the right: the base the stimulus acts on. Read together they make a Z with a stroke through it — zinc, and a measurement mark.

The proportion is fixed at 9:8, wider than it is tall, so the mark sits as an object rather than a letter. Nothing inside it is decoration: the silhouette is one closed path, which is why it survives at 16 pixels and in a single woven thread.

It is a vessel

The shape has an inside, and the inside can hold something. That is what makes the whole motion system possible, and it is why the mark is never filled with a flat colour: what fills it is a material.

The reveal

The red is found, not shown

The mark is red because muscle is, and woven in carbon because that is what repetition makes: many small fibres, one strong object.

At rest it is Zinc Silver on Night, Obsidian on Day. Point at it and a liquid rises inside the silhouette and uncovers the artwork. The brand's whole argument, performed in one interaction: the thing that matters is the thing you only notice when you look.

05

Colour

The system is called Deep Carbon: a night ground with a crimson bloom behind the headline, the diagonal cut in silver over oxblood, crimson where you act, and one live teal.

The four materials

Night. Obsidian #0D0D0D is the ground the brand speaks from. Not pure black: black is a hole, obsidian is a surface, and it keeps the mark's edge. A crimson bloom sits behind the headline — the one place light is allowed to glow on the dark.

Metal. Zinc Wash and Zinc Silver carry the words; Lid Silver #80878D carries the structure — the cut, rules and plates. It is the jar lid's own value, measured at L 62% and set in the brand's cool band.

Crimson, where you act. The mark's red leads the buttons, the drop and the product fill — on Night only. It is the brand's energy, spent on the things a person touches.

One live colour. The elixir teal means alive, measured, recovering, correct: you are here, success, the pour. It never competes with crimson because it never does crimson's job.

The ratio: 90 / 7 / 3

Night Metal 7% crimson 3% teal

Measured across a whole screen, not per element. Crimson belongs to what you act on — one primary button, the drop, a product fill. If a screen has crimson in two places that are not actions, one of them is wrong.

Two surfaces: Night and Day

The brand argues in the dark and proves itself in the light. It is also practical: Bangladesh first means mid-range Android in daylight, where long reading and dense tables on near-black are punishing.

The decision this edition settles

Red acts — but only in the dark.

Crimson leads the things a person touches: the primary button, the drop, the product filling. That was rejected while the brand was on a light ground, and the ground is what changed. Five measurements, and the four rules they force.

1 · The collision was a light-surface problem

On Day, the failure colour #B0332B sits 0.033 from the mark's crimson — the same colour at a glance, so “join the list” would read like “this went wrong”. On Night the failure colour is #FF7A6B, 0.208 away: two steps lighter and visibly different. Red can lead here because the ground moved.

2 · It fills, it does not letter

Crimson on Obsidian is 3.35:1, which fails at every size. So crimson is a fill carrying Zinc Wash at 4.9:1, and crimson type appears only at display size in the lighter tint #E87A82 (7:1).

3 · Silver is what keeps us out of the aisle

Black and red is the supplement aisle's uniform, and the diagonal stripes were the part that read as ASRV. In the lid's own silver they read as metal instead, and our crimson already carries a third less chroma than the shelf (0.144 against 0.213).

4 · Red means act, never wrong

One man in twelve is red-green blind: simulated, our pass and fail land 0.076 apart. Since red now means “act”, every error carries a word and a shape, and failure never relies on being red.

5 · On Day, red stands down

Specification surfaces keep red for the mark and for failure only. Their actions are Obsidian filling with the elixir, as today's site already does. Red works where it glows.

The four rules

One crimson action per screen. Crimson never carries small text. Every error carries a word. The mark's reveal stays the only place red is the material rather than the action.

How loud is our red, actually

Chroma in OKLCH, on one scale from 0 to 0.25. The mark's crimson carries about a third less chroma than the reds it would be shelved beside: it is an oxide, not a flame.

Where red is allowed

Allowed

  • The primary button on Night — one per screen.
  • The product filling: a tee, a tub, the drop's key moment.
  • The headline's liquid, at display size, in the lighter tint #E87A82.
  • The mark's artwork, uncovered by the reveal, and the tab icon.
  • Physical finishes: crimson thread, anodised edges, print on black.
  • The fail token, at interface scale, always beside a word.

Not allowed

  • Any action on a Day surface — there red means failure only.
  • Small text, labels, captions, links in running copy.
  • Full-bleed red panels or packaging fields. Crimson fills controls, not rooms.
  • A second crimson element on a screen that is not an action.
  • Charts and data marks, where hue would be the only cue.
  • Anything meaning “hurry”: sale flags, countdowns, urgency.

Why teal, and why only one

It is in the source material. The mood board is near-black almost everywhere. Exactly one electric colour event sits in it: a teal-green car against night asphalt.

It sits opposite the mark. Crimson reads at hue 356°, the elixir at 171° — five degrees off a true opposite. The one colour we use and the one colour the object is made of never compete; each makes the other look more like itself.

It is what zinc does. Mineral in solution, water, recovery. Metal is zinc on a shelf; the elixir is zinc once it is inside you.

It has a local root. The Bangladesh flag's green #006A4E sits 0.023 away in OKLab and 6.5° of hue from our Elixir. A neighbour, never a flag.

One accent, not two. A second brand colour is how systems lose their meaning. Info blue and the pass / warn / fail trio are interface tools for instruments and forms, not brand colours.

Off the screen

Fabric. Garment dye mutes colour: match crimson thread and teal stitching to a physical swatch before signing, never to the hex. Buy the swatch first.

Print. Obsidian prints as a rich black, not 100% K alone. The elixir is a spot colour when the budget allows — teal is the first thing four-colour process gets wrong.

Cheap screens. Deep crimson and Elixir void both crush toward black on low-end panels in daylight. Never put information in the difference between two dark values; the level line carries it instead.

Never colour alone. Every state that colour marks also carries a word or a shape — a dot with a label, a pass with the word pass.

06

Type

One family, pushed to both extremes. Width carries the hierarchy, so a second typeface is never needed.

Why one family

Two earlier passes were wrong and the corrections are worth keeping. A wide grotesque was tried first — but two grams of zinc is concentrated, not spread out, so display had to compress rather than expand. A high-contrast serif was tried next, which is elegant, and elegance is not the same thing as energy.

Archivo is variable in both weight and width. Narrow and heavy, it hits at display size; at normal width it reads quietly for paragraphs. Most brands pair a display face with a body face and get two typefaces having a polite disagreement.

Do

  • Keep the measure under 70 characters. Long lines make white space read as neglect.
  • Let width carry hierarchy: compress to shout, relax to explain.
  • Set Bangla beside English at the same level, using the same width logic.
  • Use Space Mono wherever a number must be checkable.

Don't

  • No third typeface, ever.
  • No all-caps in Archivo. Uppercase survives only in Space Mono at small sizes, where it reads as instrument lettering.
  • No painting one word of a headline a different colour. If the line needs help, rewrite the line — colour may only arrive as the substance filling the type.
  • No italics for emphasis in display. Rewrite instead.

07

Motion and states

Everything fills because a body fills: blood moving, a dose landing, a level rising toward ready.

Why it works

It is literally true of the subject. Zinc arrives dissolved and travels in fluid. A dose is a level. Every category we sell is a vessel: a tee takes on sweat, a tub dissolves into a glass, an instrument reads a level. Nothing has to be invented to make the interface mean something.

And it solves the brand problem: restraint is hard to make exciting. A filling liquid gives energy from substance rather than noise — the page moves because something is being readied, not because something is being advertised.

Reveal over display

Nothing is fully visible at rest. The mark holds its material until you point at it, a headline sits half-poured, a product fills as you consider it. Attention is the thing the brand rewards.

Two grammars, and only two

GrammarMeansWhere
DotsWhere you areNavigation, current page, live state, a signal beside a word
LiquidWhat you doButtons, the mark, products, the pour, reading progress

If a new component needs motion, it must be one of these two. A third grammar is a sign the component is wrong, not that the system is short.

Rules

Nothing loops.No drifting, pulsing or floating. A loop is decoration, and decoration undermines restraint.
One arrival moment.A single settling gesture on load. Fading every section up on scroll is the generated-page default.
Motion answers a person.Hover, focus, press. On a touch screen the middle line of the screen stands in for the cursor, and scrolling may drive a pour — progress, never playback.
Gradients live inside liquid.A liquid is a volume with light travelling through it. A surface is a surface: zinc is a solid, and a gradient on a button or a card would be the first dishonest thing on the page.
Reduced motion removes movement, never information.With prefers-reduced-motion, or with no JavaScript, every section shows its finished state.

Two setups, with targets

Targets are for the surfaces as the camera sees them, not for paint. One rule holds both: every surface stays in the cool band (hue 200–260°) or under 0.010 chroma. Never 60–100° above 0.012 — that single line is the whole difference between the render's room and this brand.

The mark is the only colour in shot, about 2% of the pixels. Plates and rack fall into shadow rather than being lit. For home, Origin, the drop, campaign and social.

The render is one grade away from the second setup. Same framing, same props minus the tile floor: swap cream stone for cool concrete and pull the white balance cool, and it belongs.

Do

  • Near-black sets, one hard light source, deep falloff — at least half the frame below L 30%.
  • Cool light: steel, water, chalk dust, wet concrete, night asphalt.
  • Keep every surface in the cool band, or under 0.010 chroma.
  • Light the product like a tool on a bench, not a trophy on a plinth.
  • Real gyms and real light in Dhaka. The origin is the asset.
  • Let the red appear only where it actually is: the object's own finish.
  • Leave room for the mark and its clear space when shooting.

Don't

  • No orange gym lighting, no warm grade, no teal-and-orange. Cream stone is the warm the brand already rejected.
  • No prop that repeats what the pack already says once — one Zn, not a floor of them.
  • No generated image with garbled micro-text on a label, a tile or a spec panel. Redraw the type or reshoot.
  • No grimacing, veins, or mid-rep screaming.
  • No motivational type burned into a photograph.
  • No stock-looking studio white sweeps for brand images — those belong on Day surfaces, in a spec panel.
  • No red gels, filters or coloured smoke.

11

Keeping it

Fixed

Changing any of these is a brand decision, not a design one: the name and its spelling; the mark's geometry, proportion and clear space; the two surfaces; one live colour; red as material rather than paint; the type family; dots and liquid as the only two grammars; nothing loops.

Still open

Photography — nothing shot yet. Instruments, until there is a real product behind the door. Bangla at display sizes. A spot-colour match for the elixir in print, and a crimson thread swatch for Wear. Whether the homepage goes Night immediately or at the first drop.

Settled in this edition

Deep Carbon is the system, cut in both. The base is unchanged — the bloom behind the headline, crimson where you act, teal for live states. The diagonal is new: Lid Silver leading, Oxblood behind. On Day surfaces red means failure only, so no crimson actions there.

It is built. The site carries it: .night and .day set the tokens, .has-cut carries the diagonal, and the style guide documents both.

The elixir rule is refined. “Never on light” was written for Elixir Lit and above (2.2:1 and worse). The Elixir core measures 5.4:1 on Zinc Wash and may carry small type there.

Uppercase is allowed in Space Mono only, at small sizes, where it reads as instrument lettering.

No glow behind type. The lab's crimson bloom is not adopted: surfaces stay flat, and light stays inside liquid.

How to change something

Every value in this book has a reason. If a reason stops being true, change the value — and write down which reason died. Proposals live in the decision log with the measurement that makes the case; nothing is built from a proposal.

The colour lab is where directions get tried before they get proposed: five directions on the same brand pieces, a mixer for building new ones, and contrast measured live. It is a workbench, not a spec — this book is the spec.
